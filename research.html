Current work

Research

My research asks how post-training modifies factual knowledge, why unrelated knowledge is sometimes destabilized, and how those effects accumulate under sequential model updates.

Research agenda

I approach post-training knowledge dynamics from three complementary levels: the training objective used to constrain an update, the token-level predictive signals that anticipate degradation, and the layer-wise transformations through which the model changes its computation. The long-term goal is to make repeated knowledge updates both reliable and interpretable.

01

KLOD

Locality-Preserving Knowledge Editing via Non-Target Distribution Preservation

Motivation

Fine-tuning-based knowledge editing can achieve high edit success while shifting unrelated predictions. These side effects become more consequential when edits are applied sequentially, because small distributional changes can accumulate across updates and cause locality degradation.

Central idea

KLOD combines a bounded target logit-odds objective with two distribution-preserving regularizers. It stops target amplification once a probability threshold is reached, preserves the target-excluded non-target distribution at target positions, and preserves the full next-token distribution at prefix positions.

Research scope

The project covers objective design, baseline comparisons, sequential editing protocols, locality evaluation, ablation studies, and distributional KL analyses on CounterFact and ZsRE with Llama3-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. I led method implementation, experimental design, result analysis, and technical writing.

Current status

Accepted to Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EMNLP 2026. The paper will appear in the Findings proceedings.

02

Target-Token Prior

A predictor of forgetting in fine-tuning-based knowledge editing

Research question

Knowledge edited with the same optimization procedure can exhibit very different levels of degradation. This project asks whether that variation can be anticipated from the probability assigned to the target token before the update.

Diagnostic and intervention

We analyze target-token prior probability as a sample-level predictor of forgetting and test an intervention that first raises the target fact’s pre-update probability before applying fine-tuning-based editing.

Key transition

The intervention mitigated forgetting in single-edit settings but did not remain consistently reliable under sequential updates. This limitation suggested that preserving the broader predictive distribution—not only increasing target probability—was necessary, directly motivating KLOD.

Research role

I led the post-diagnosis method development, implementation, subsequent experimental design, and result analysis, and co-wrote the manuscript as the first-listed co-first author.

03

Early-Stage Forgetting and Layer-Wise Dynamics

Ongoing mechanistic analysis of post-training transformations

Motivation

Severe forgetting can be concentrated in the earliest stages of fine-tuning, including under objectives intended to preserve predictive distributions. This suggests that output-level constraints alone may not explain how damaging internal changes arise.

Current questions

  • Which layers exhibit the earliest and largest transformations during post-training?
  • Can those changes be decomposed into rotational and scaling components?
  • How do representation subspaces and computational pathways relate to changes in the output distribution?

Current analysis

I am examining layer-wise transformations from rotational and scaling perspectives and studying how these transformations interact with the subspaces used to express target and non-target knowledge.
