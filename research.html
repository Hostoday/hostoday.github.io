Current work

Research

My current work examines why post-training changes some knowledge reliably while destabilizing other knowledge, and how those dynamics accumulate under sequential updates.

KLOD

Locality-Preserving Knowledge Editing via Non-Target Distribution Preservation

Fine-tuning-based editing can achieve high edit success while changing unrelated predictions. The problem becomes more difficult when multiple edits are applied sequentially.

Approach

KLOD uses a KL-based objective to preserve the distribution over non-target tokens during knowledge updates. The aim is to reduce locality degradation without sacrificing the intended edit.

Contribution

Led method development, implementation, baseline selection, experimental design, ablation studies, and result analysis; drafted the initial manuscript structure and core technical content.

Submitted to ARR · May 2026

Target-Token Prior

A predictor of forgetting in fine-tuning-based knowledge editing

This project asks whether knowledge degradation after an edit can be anticipated from the model’s pre-update probability assigned to the target token.

Approach

We analyze target-token prior probability as a diagnostic signal and test an intervention that raises the target knowledge’s pre-update probability before fine-tuning.

Finding and transition

The intervention was useful in single-edit settings but was not consistently reliable under sequential updates. That limitation directly motivated the KLOD project.

Submitted to ARR · May 2026

Early-Stage Forgetting and Layer-Wise Dynamics

Ongoing mechanistic analysis of post-training transformations

Severe forgetting can concentrate in the earliest stages of fine-tuning, including under objectives intended to preserve predictive distributions.

Current analysis

I am examining layer-wise transformations from rotational and scaling perspectives, and studying connections among representation subspaces, output distributions, and computational pathways used to express knowledge.
