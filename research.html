Research

Research

My research focuses on how post-training changes factual knowledge in large language models, how knowledge can be retained or forgotten across sequential updates, and how these changes relate to internal representations.

Research agenda

I study knowledge dynamics at three complementary levels: editing objectives that constrain unintended output changes, token-level signals that characterize update demand and forgetting, and representation geometry that helps distinguish different forms of internal drift. My ongoing work examines early-stage forgetting and layer-wise transformations during fine-tuning.

01 · EMNLP 2026 Findings

KLOD

Locality-Preserving Knowledge Editing via Non-Target Distribution Preservation

Motivation

Cross-entropy-based knowledge editing can increase the probability of an intended target while also changing the probabilities of unrelated outputs. In sequential editing, such non-target distribution drift can accumulate and damage locality even when edits remain reliable.

Approach

KLOD combines bounded target logit-odds optimization with distribution-preserving KL constraints. It limits further target amplification after a probability threshold is reached, preserves the target-excluded distribution at target positions, and constrains the full next-token distribution at prefix positions.

Selected results

  • On LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct / CounterFact, improved the locality score from 1.87 to 44.75 while retaining 99.70 reliability.
  • On zsRE, achieved 86.47 locality and 99.89 reliability.
  • Evaluated sequential knowledge editing up to 150,000 edits across a broader experimental study involving two model backbones and two datasets.

My contribution and status

I led the implementation, baseline comparisons, experimental design, and ablation studies. The paper was accepted to Findings of EMNLP 2026 and is to appear.

02 · Manuscript under review

PLFT-KE

Probability-Lift Fine-Tuning for Knowledge Editing: Target-Token Prior as a Signal of Update Demand

Research question

Why do some facts degrade more than others during fine-tuning-based knowledge editing? In a controlled synthetic biography setting with 50,000 pre-training and 50,000 fine-tuning individuals, we found that the target-token prior was more informative about forgetting than general output-token confidence in that setting.

Approach

PLFT-KE introduces a two-stage probability-lift initialization. It first optimizes a latent shift while keeping the model frozen, converts that shift into a rank-1 weight update, and then consolidates the change through standard fine-tuning.

Selected results and limitations

  • On LLaMA-3.2-3B / CounterFact, increased the FT-M score from 58.43 to 72.41.
  • Improved specificity from 33.55 to 59.40 in the same reported evaluation.
  • Improvements were weaker and heterogeneous in long sequential-editing settings; the single-edit gains should not be interpreted as consistent benefits across extended sequences.

Status

Co-first-authored manuscript under review at Expert Systems with Applications.

03 · Manuscript under review

Beyond Norm Growth

A Geometric Decomposition of Hidden-State Drift in Sequential Knowledge Editing

Research question

Hidden states can change after sequential knowledge edits, but an increase in displacement or norm alone does not identify the geometry of that change. This work studies how much of the displacement from a base model reflects parallel movement versus orthogonal movement.

Approach

I decomposed base-relative hidden-state displacement into parallel and orthogonal components across models, datasets, and editing conditions. Controlled hidden-state interventions were compared with parallel controls matched for the same final norm to examine the contribution of geometric direction.

Selected findings

  • At the measured boundary, the checkpoint-median orthogonal share of displacement energy was approximately 98.7–99.3%.
  • Across 34 eligible endpoint states, a 25% reduction of the orthogonal component produced mean changes of +0.729 percentage points in locality and +0.333 percentage points in teacher-forced efficacy.
  • The intervention effects varied across states; these mean changes should not be interpreted as uniform improvements.

Status

Manuscript under review at an MDPI journal.

04 · Ongoing research

Early-Stage Forgetting and Layer-Wise Transformations

Connecting representation subspaces, output distributions, and factual recall

Current direction

I am investigating forgetting during the early stages of fine-tuning and analyzing layer-wise rotational and scaling changes. The aim is to understand how transformations in internal representations relate to shifts in predictive distributions and the model's ability to recall facts.

Questions under investigation

  • How do rotational and scaling changes evolve across layers during early fine-tuning?
  • Which representation-space changes coincide with changes in factual recall?
  • How are layer-wise transformations reflected in the output distribution?
