NLP · Large Language Models

Hojun Jeong

M.S. Student in Artificial Intelligence at Gachon University

I am a graduate researcher in the ISNLP Lab at Gachon University, advised by Prof. Sangwoo Kang.

My research focuses on how factual knowledge is updated, retained, and forgotten in large language models. I am interested in knowledge editing, sequential model updates, representation dynamics, and mechanistic interpretability.

My current projects investigate locality-preserving knowledge editing (KLOD), target-prior-aware fine-tuning (PLFT-KE), and the geometry of hidden-state drift (Beyond Norm Growth). I also study early-stage forgetting and layer-wise transformations during fine-tuning. Earlier, I worked on dialogue summarization.

KLOD · Locality-preserving knowledge editing

PLFT-KE · Target-prior-aware fine-tuning

PLFT-KE uses a probability-lift initialization to study and mitigate forgetting. It improves single-edit results in the reported evaluation, while showing weaker and heterogeneous gains under long sequential editing. Project details →

Beyond Norm Growth · Hidden-state drift

EMNLP · Findings 2026

KLOD: Locality-Preserving Knowledge Editing via Non-Target Distribution Preservation

Hojun Jeong*, Gyunyeop Kim*, and Sangwoo Kang

Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EMNLP 2026 · Accepted / To appear · *Equal contribution

PDF Code

Proposes a bounded target logit-odds objective and distribution-preserving KL constraints to mitigate locality degradation in sequential knowledge editing while maintaining high reliability. On LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct / CounterFact, locality improves from 1.87 to 44.75 with 99.70 reliability; experiments include sequences of up to 150,000 edits.

ESWA · Under review

Probability-Lift Fine-Tuning for Knowledge Editing: Target-Token Prior as a Signal of Update Demand

Hojun Jeong*, Gyunyeop Kim*, and Sangwoo Kang

Under review at Expert Systems with Applications · *Equal contribution

Studies target-token prior probability as a signal of update demand and proposes a two-stage probability-lift initialization before fine-tuning. On LLaMA-3.2-3B / CounterFact, the FT-M score improves from 58.43 to 72.41 and specificity from 33.55 to 59.40. Improvements under long sequential editing are weaker and heterogeneous.

Beyond Norm Growth: A Geometric Decomposition of Hidden-State Drift in Sequential Knowledge Editing

Hojun Jeong and Sangwoo Kang

Under review at an MDPI journal

Decomposes base-relative hidden-state displacement into parallel and orthogonal components to distinguish representation drift from norm growth. Orthogonal components account for approximately 98.7–99.3% of checkpoint-median displacement energy at the measured boundary. Controlled interventions show small, heterogeneous changes in locality and teacher-forced efficacy.
